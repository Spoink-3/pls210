Does Club Membership Predict Institutionalized Political Participation?

RQ Positioning Contribution Literature Theory AI Use Reflection References

Research Question

Among students at Kazakhstani universities, does membership in a student-led club (vs. non-membership) predict institutionalized political participation — voting, party membership — measured Fall 2026 to Fall 2027?
Panel design · same students surveyed twice, one year apart

Positioning: Positivist

  • Testing one specific hypothesis, not interpreting meaning
  • Variables are measurable and observable: membership (yes/no), voting (yes/no), party membership (yes/no)
  • Goal: a generalizable causal pattern across many students, not one group's lived experience

Contribution — the Gap

  • No Central Asian study exists on this relationship — confirmed by a systematic search returning zero results
  • Closest prior work studies fraternities/sororities — selective, socially exclusive orgs with their own selection bias, not open-enrollment clubs
  • Some prior work studies designed interventions (mock elections, facilitated debates) — this study observes clubs governing themselves with no researcher involvement
  • Panel design (same students, two waves) gives more leverage on reverse causality than most existing cross-sectional work

Literature Review

Inner tier

Kumler & Whittaker (2020)

"...a third group of 'nudged' debate watchers voted and registered at higher rates than nonwatchers." (abstract)

Fraternity/sorority members, reference-group theory. Supports the peer-influence mechanism — but Greek-specific, US context.

Inner tier

Bowman, Park & Denson (2015)

"...students are quite predisposed to participate (or not participate) in these organizations, which can lead to significant challenges..." (abstract)

Names the exact selection problem this study faces. Uses statistical matching, not a panel, to address it.

Theory

Levine (2016)

"Perhaps sports participation and civic engagement reflect other factors such as an energetic and outgoing personality, better schools, or more supportive families."

States the rival "common cause" hypothesis this study must address plainly.

Theory

Bobkowski & Miller (2016)

"...taking journalism classes is related positively to voting... to a similar degree that taking debate classes or participating in student government is related to voting." (abstract)

Nationally representative, longitudinal — closest methodological model for this study.

Theory

Syvertsen et al. (2009)

"...significant effects of the program on students' self-reported ability to cast an informed vote... belief that their vote matters..." (abstract)

A designed civics intervention, not organic club life — measures self-reported dispositions, not confirmed voting behavior.

Theory & Hypothesis

IV
Club membership — binary (0 = non-member, 1 = member)
DV
Institutionalized political participation — voted in last election (Y/N); party member (Y/N)
H1
Club members show higher institutionalized political participation than non-members.

Mechanism 1 — Small-scale democratic practice

Membership → practicing club governance (electing/replacing leaders) → political self-efficacy ↑ → participation ↑

Mechanism 2 — Peer influence (reference group)

Membership → peers treat voting as normal/expected → social pressure to conform → participation ↑

Rival explanation, addressed

Same trait could cause both (Levine, 2016). Panel design checks whether change in membership lines up with change in participation — doesn't eliminate selection bias, but gives leverage on it.
